\begin{abstract}
Fog service placement under burst load and membership churn is hard when the
control plane itself must form from scratch. Most learning placers either assume
an already organized hierarchy or score hosts over a near-global action set, so
control cost and cold-start behavior are rarely isolated. We present
\textbf{DynaCol-GNN}, a colony-bounded hybrid placer that keeps DynaCol's
cold-start formation, FCM handover, CRT/GRT tables, sticky L1 reuse, and cloud
L3 fallback, and changes only L2 host ranking. Candidates stay inside the pruned
CRT (with optional GRT escalation). Online GraphSAGE or flat-vector scorers mix
with the deterministic DCBO objective through a scale-aware blend that returns
to exact DCBO when the fog count is large. In iFogSim2, a 20-trial multipipe
journal grid shows both hybrids reduce mean SLA versus DCBO at matched overhead
on normal, burst, and churn loads, with Holm-significant paired gaps and
bootstrap CIs that exclude zero. Encoder ranking between GraphSAGE and the
vector ablation is mixed and non-significant. A same-plane tabular
$\varepsilon$-greedy learner is a strong SLA control inside the colony plane;
flat baselines (FogPlan, Greedy-Nearest, Static-DCBO) give absolute SLA context
outside that plane. At $N{=}1000$ the methods stay close with matched overhead.
\end{abstract}

fog computing, service placement, graph neural networks, hybrid learning,

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Service placement at the fog and edge must keep end-to-end delay inside SLA
limits while demand, membership, and link quality move
\cite{chiang_jiot2016,yousefpour_sysarc2019}.
When load bursts and nodes leave, placers that need a global snapshot, or that
assume a ready hierarchy, either spend too much on control or fail early in the
run \cite{salaht_csur2020,taleb_csur2025_survey}.
Reinforcement learning and related adapters help under changing demand
\cite{supraja_cluster2025_slr,schulman_ppo2017}, yet many designs still open the
action set to almost every host and rarely report the messaging cost of that
choice \cite{hong_csur2019_resource}.

DynaCol already covers the control-plane side: colonies form from a zero-colony
state, an FCM is elected and handed over, and CRT/GRT tables stay bounded. DCBO
is the default L2 scorer, with sticky L1 reuse and cloud L3 fallback. Missing is
a learner that is \emph{forced} to pick inside those same bounded views.

We leave formation, handover, CRT/GRT, L1, and L3 as in DynaCol and replace only
L2 ranking. At each decision a GraphSAGE network scores at most the top-$K$ CRT
hosts (RTT affinity plus service-DAG context), mixes that score with inverted
DCBO utility, and reduces the learning blend to zero once the fabric is large
enough that exact DCBO is preferred (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). The vector policy is
the same MDP without message passing (Fig.~\ref{fig:encoders}).

The MDP is colony-bounded ($|A_t|\le K$ after cold start), so the control
plane is part of the learning method. L2 hybrids online scoring with DCBO and
hands back to exact DCBO at large $N$. We compare a HID$=$16 GraphSAGE encoder
(offline import plus fine-tune) with a flat vector ablation and a same-plane
tabular $\varepsilon$-greedy learner on iFogSim2 under normal/burst/churn,
including a 20-trial journal grid with Holm tests, bootstrap CIs, and Cohen's
$d$, an $N{=}1000$ check, one-factor sensitivity, and named flat baselines for
absolute context.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{C1 --- Colony-bounded MDP.} Formal state/action/reward with
$|A_t|\le K_{\mathrm{crt}}$ after cold start.
\item \textbf{C2 --- Hybrid L2 stack.} Online scoring hybridized with DCBO plus
scale-aware blend and exact large-scale DCBO delegation.
\item \textbf{C3 --- Encoders and same-plane learner.} GraphSAGE with offline
import and fine-tune; vector ablation; tabular DRL control on the same CRT plane.
\item \textbf{C4 --- Empirical study.} Locked multipipe journal $n{=}20$ with
Holm/CI/effect sizes, sensitivity, $N{=}1000$ parity, and Pareto SLA--overhead
context against flat baselines.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:rw}

\subsection{Fog and edge service placement}
Surveys of fog placement cover latency, energy, and cost under fixed or
centralized controllers
\cite{salaht_csur2020,smolka_computing2022_eval,taleb_csur2025_survey,apat_computers2025_survey,chiang_jiot2016,yousefpour_sysarc2019}.
Heuristic and metaheuristic assigners, FogTorch-style QoS matching, and FogPlan
Min-Cost assignment improve mapping quality on a given topology
\cite{skarlat_icfec2017,azimzadeh_cluster2022_genetic,benamer_cluster2024_ga,brogi_jiot2017_fogtorch,yousefpour_fogplan_jiot2019},
but they do not form a self-organizing overlay from an empty colony state.

\subsection{Learning-based offloading and placement}
DQN/PPO-style and other learning placers adapt online
\cite{mnih_nature2015_dqn,schulman_ppo2017,supraja_cluster2025_slr},
with applications in healthcare/fog stacks, energy-aware modules, and multi-tier
containers
\cite{tuli_iotj2020_dynamic,hossam_cluster2024_energy,dogani_cluster2024_twotier}.
Resource-management surveys still note large action spaces and the assumption
of an organized fabric
\cite{hong_csur2019_resource,costa_csur2022,pallewatta_csur2023_microservices}.
DynaCol-GNN keeps the action set inside CRT/GRT top-$K$ and mixes learning with
DCBO so cold start stays stable.

\subsection{Self-organizing and hierarchical fog control}
Overlay formation and manager election (including DynaCol) separate structure
from placement. Hierarchical module managers and evolutionary mappers improve
QoS once a hierarchy exists
\cite{mahmud_fog_application2018,guerrero_fgcs2019_evolutionary},
but learning is seldom constrained to the colony view as a hard limit. Here
formation stays with DynaCol; learning only re-ranks inside that view.

\subsection{Graph learning on infrastructure and service graphs}
GraphSAGE and GNN-RL placers encode dependency graphs for scheduling and
multi-objective mapping \cite{hamilton_graphsage2017,lera_jsup2024_gnn}.
CRT neighborhoods are small and RTT-structured, so local message passing fits,
provided the action set stays colony-bounded. On our multipipe champion lock the
GNN beats the vector ablation; we do not claim a large gap on every grid.

\subsection{Relation to DynaCol}
DynaCol/DCBO is the prior control plane and baseline
\cite{nabati_dynacol_cluster2026}. We do not restate cold-start formation or
FCM handover as new results; while that manuscript is under review, the reused
algorithms are reprinted in Appendix~\ref{app:dynacol} for self-containment.
The deltas here are the colony-bounded L2 MDP, the hybrid GraphSAGE/vector
learners with scale-aware blend, a same-plane tabular learner baseline, and the
multipipe / journal / baseline study.

\section{Background: DynaCol Control Plane}
\label{sec:bg}

Only the reused pieces are summarized here
\cite{nabati_dynacol_cluster2026}; pseudocode appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:dynacol}.

Nodes join by query/advertise until colonies appear. An FCM is elected by
potency and updated with $\varepsilon$-greedy probes and a cooldown on handover.
Each FCM keeps a CRT of member capacity, availability, RTT, and attractiveness
(default top-$K_{\mathrm{crt}}{=}5$); GRT summaries allow limited cross-colony
search (default top-$K_{\mathrm{grt}}{=}3$) without a full host list.
Placement is three-tier: sticky L1 reuse if the current host still meets SLA
thresholds; DCBO L2 scoring over CRT then GRT; cloud L3 if both fail.
DynaCol-GNN replaces only the L2 ranking inside CRT/GRT top-$K$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\section{Colony-Bounded Hybrid Learning}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{MDP}
On each FCM reconcile, state $S_t$ is the local CRT (size $\le K_{\mathrm{crt}}$),
optional GRT summaries, and service/DAG features. Action $A_t$ picks a feasible
CRT host or escalates to GRT/L3. The hard limit $|A_t|\le K_{\mathrm{crt}}$ is
intentional: the control plane is part of the learning method.

Host features (dimension 8) are normalized CPU, RAM, storage, bandwidth, RTT to
FCM, attractiveness, a neighbor-message channel, and producer-host affinity.
Context (dimension 8) concatenates service/DAG scalars, producer-RTT pull,
fan-in, and a co-location indicator. The vector ablation zeros the co-location
channel so message passing and structure-weighted context are the main encoder
differences.

\paragraph{Reward.}
Let $r_{\mathrm{attr}}$ be the attractiveness reward used by DCBO and
$r_{\mathrm{dcbo}}$ the same signal at the DCBO-preferred CRT host. The shaped
online target is
\begin{equation}
R_t=\mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\bigl(0.5+0.5(r_{\mathrm{attr}}-r_{\mathrm{dcbo}})\bigr).
\end{equation}
For GNN, a graph-SLA score (producer-RTT pull, co-location under fan-in) further
biases the contrastive update. Vector uses the same $R_t$ with a weak linear
TD-style update on the chosen feature vector.

\subsection{Hybrid scoring}
\begin{equation}
s(i)=u_{\mathrm{DCBO}}(i)+\beta\cdot f_\theta\bigl(\phi(i)\bigr),
\label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
with $u_{\mathrm{DCBO}}$ the inverted DCBO objective. We take $\arg\max_i s(i)$
and allow a small $\varepsilon$-greedy rate while $n_f$ is below the large-scale
gate. The blend $\beta$ falls with fog count (default $0.60$ on multipipe at
small $N$) and is set to zero for $n_f\ge 450$, so L2 becomes exact DCBO.

\subsection{Encoders}
The vector policy (\texttt{colony\_rl}) concatenates host channels with
service/DAG scalars and scores with a linear head (learning rate $0.05$). The
GNN policy (\texttt{colony\_gnn}) builds an RTT-affinity graph on CRT candidates
with $A_{ij}\propto 1/(1+\mathrm{RTT}_{ij}/20)$ and a two-layer GraphSAGE-style
update (hidden width $16$)
\begin{equation}
h'=\tanh\bigl(W_{\mathrm{self}}h+W_{\mathrm{neigh}}Ah\bigr),
\end{equation}
then reads out from $\mathrm{concat}(h,\mathrm{context})$ (learning rate
$0.04$). Offline JSON weights can be loaded and fine-tuned online
(Fig.~\ref{fig:encoders}).

A same-plane tabular baseline (\texttt{drl}) keeps cold-start CRT/GRT formation
but replaces L2 with $\varepsilon$-greedy selection on attractiveness
($\varepsilon{=}0.15$), without a neural encoder or DCBO hybridization. It is
not a deep Q-network; we report it as a lightweight learning control inside the
colony plane.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig02_encoders.pdf}
\caption{Vector ablation and GraphSAGE pipelines into the same hybrid score
\eqref{eq:hybrid}.}
\label{fig:encoders}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Online updates and offline import}
After $i^\star$ is chosen, GNN applies a centered advantage update
$R_t-\bar R$ with a soft contrastive push toward the graph-SLA-best CRT index
when available; Vector applies a delta rule on the chosen features toward
$R_t$. Attractiveness decay/update mirrors DCBO.

\paragraph{Offline path.}
Exploratory \texttt{colony\_gnn} runs log CRT tensors (host matrix, context,
adjacency, action, reward, optional DCBO base scores). Outside the simulator we
train a GIN-style encoder matching the Java layout with
\textbf{reward-advantage behavioral cloning plus structure ranking} (not classic
PPO): weighted NLL on the logged action, softplus ranking between
structure-best/worst hosts, an optional ranking term against the inverted DCBO
base vector, and a small entropy bonus. Exported JSON tensors load into the
online GNN; champion and journal grids enable online fine-tuning.

\subsection{Cost and algorithm}
Scoring touches at most $K_{\mathrm{crt}}$ candidates at fixed feature/hidden
width---$O(K_{\mathrm{crt}})$ per decision with no global host enumeration.
Messaging remains the CRT/GRT traffic of DynaCol.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Colony-bounded hybrid reconcile (sketch)}
\label{alg:reconcile}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Procedure{Reconcile}{$r$, fcm, current}
  \If{$\beta\approx 0$}
    \State \Return DCBO.reconcile($\ldots$)
  \EndIf
  \If{L1 sticky OK}
    \State \Return current
  \EndIf
  \If{CRT enabled}
    \State $C\leftarrow$ top-$K$ feasible CRT entries
    \State pick $i^\star$ by hybrid score on $\phi(C)$; update $\theta$
    \State \Return host($i^\star$)
  \EndIf
  \If{GRT enabled}
    \State escalate via ranked GRT summaries
  \EndIf
  \State \Return cloud \Comment{L3}
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Simulations run in iFogSim2 on the shared DynaCol runtime
\cite{gupta_ifogsim2017,mahmud_ifogsim2_jss2022}.
We compare \texttt{dcbo}, \texttt{colony\_rl} (Vector),
\texttt{colony\_gnn} (GNN), and same-plane tabular \texttt{drl}, plus flat
\texttt{static\_dcbo}, \texttt{fogplan}, and \texttt{greedy}. Edgeward was
dropped on multipipe after single trials exceeded 25 minutes.

Target sizes are $N\in\{100,300,500,1000\}$ under normal, burst, and churn.
The surveillance DAG is the shallow camera pipeline; the multipipe DAG adds
fan-out, skip, and join edges (Fig.~\ref{fig:dag}) with SLA deadline scale $10$.
We report SLA violation \%, P95 loop delay, normalized control overhead, and
colony count. The tuning lock uses 5 trials; the journal $N{=}100$ grid uses
\textbf{20} trials with paired $t$, Holm adjustment, bootstrap CIs, and Cohen's
$d$ \cite{holm_1979}; $N{=}1000$ uses 10 trials; flat baselines use 5 trials.
One-factor sensitivity sweeps ($\beta$, deadline scale, $\varepsilon$) use 5
Burst trials. Locked settings: HID$=$16, $\beta{=}0.60$, $\varepsilon{=}0.02$,
offline weights with fine-tune.

\subsection{Journal multipipe grid ($n{=}20$)}

result. Both hybrids beat DCBO on every load at matched overhead
($\approx$1770 normalized messages; $\approx$7.65 colonies). Paired bootstrap

Holm-adjusted tests clear $\alpha{=}0.05$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:holm}). GNN vs Vector

lowest SLA means with slightly lower overhead ($\approx$1501) at the same
colony count---a strong learning control inside the cold-start plane, not a
matched-overhead peer of the hybrids.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}

Bounding $|A_t|$ ties learning to the same CRT/GRT traffic DynaCol already
exchanges. Inside that plane, hybrid L2 improves $N{=}100$ multipipe SLA versus
DCBO with Holm-significant paired evidence at $n{=}20$
(Table~\ref{tab:journal}).

The title emphasizes \emph{colony-bounded hybrid learning} rather than a
universal GNN-over-Vector gap: encoder ranking is mixed and non-significant on the
journal grid, while GraphSAGE remains the named structured encoder with an
offline BC+ranking import path. Tabular DRL shows that a lightweight learner on
the same CRT can lead absolute SLA; the hybrid's distinctive properties are
DCBO anchoring, scale-aware exact fallback, and structure-aware offline
initialization---not raw dominance over every same-plane learner.

Flat baselines in Table~\ref{tab:base} and Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto} are useful for
absolute SLA, not for a same-plane ranking. We do not claim new cold-start
formation, new FCM election, or testbed validation. Limits include
simulation-only evidence, calibrated multipipe deadlines (Fig.~\ref{fig:sens}),
logged (not fully on-policy) offline data, and the omission of Edgeward on
multipipe for runtime reasons.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

DynaCol-GNN keeps fog placement inside cold-start colony views: L2 mixes online
scoring with DCBO, actions stay in CRT/GRT top-$K$, and large fabrics fall back
to exact DCBO. On iFogSim2 the hybrid lowers SLA at $N{=}100$ versus DCBO at
matched overhead on surveillance and multipipe workloads, with
Holm-significant multipipe gaps at $n{=}20$, and stays close at $N{=}1000$.
GraphSAGE and the vector ablation are statistically tied as encoders; a
same-plane tabular learner provides a strong SLA control. Flat baselines remind
the reader that absolute SLA and control-plane cost can trade off outside the
colony plane.

Next steps include CRT-ablated learning, stronger on-policy offline training,
deeper same-plane neural baselines, and testbed runs.

\section{Reused DynaCol algorithms}
\label{app:dynacol}

For self-containment while the prior DynaCol manuscript is under review
\cite{nabati_dynacol_cluster2026}, we reprint the cold-start, handover, and
DCBO L1--L3 skeletons that this paper leaves unchanged. These algorithms are
\emph{not} claimed as contributions here.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cold-start join (Create\_And\_Add\_FogNode)}
\label{alg:cold}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require join event for node $FID$; timers $W_t$, $T_{\mathrm{adv}}$; radius MaxColonySize
\State state $\leftarrow$ Unassigned
\State broadcast QueryColony($FID$); start timer $T$
\If{no response and $T\ge W_t$}
  \State self-elect FCM; init CRT/GRT; AdvertisePeriodically($T_{\mathrm{adv}}$)
\Else
  \State $resp\leftarrow$ best JoinScore response
  \If{$L(FID,resp.\mathrm{FCM})<$ MaxColonySize}
    \State JoinOrNegotiate($resp$) \Comment{Alg.~\ref{alg:handover}}
  \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{JoinOrNegotiate (potency-based FCM handover)}
\label{alg:handover}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require response $resp$ with colony id, FCM, CRT
\If{self is FCM-candidate and Potency(self)$>$Potency($resp$.FCM)$+\varepsilon$ and cooldown elapsed}
  \State negotiate ManagerTakeoverRequest; on accept promote self and merge CRT
\Else
  \State join $resp$.ColonyID under $resp$.FCM; FCM updates CRT/GRT
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DCBO\_PlaceService (L1--L3)}
\label{alg:dcbo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require service $s$, current host $h_{\mathrm{cur}}$, tables CRT, GRT
\If{$h_{\mathrm{cur}}$ feasible and SLA-safe}
  \State \Return $h_{\mathrm{cur}}$ \Comment{L1 sticky}
\EndIf
\State Cand $\leftarrow$ top-$K_{\mathrm{crt}}$ feasible CRT hosts by $J(s,\cdot)$
\If{Cand $\ne\emptyset$}
  \State \Return $\arg\min$ Cand \Comment{L2 local}
\EndIf
\For{all col in top-$K_{\mathrm{grt}}$(GRT)}
  \If{DelegatePlace($s$, col.FCM) succeeds}
    \State \Return delegated host \Comment{L2 neighbor}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return cloud by latency/cost \Comment{L3}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
